Search for the best fulfillment company for Shopify in Canada and you will find plenty of ranked lists. Many of them are written by the companies being ranked, or by sites paid when you sign up. A list cannot tell you which provider fits your store. A good set of questions can. This guide explains what to compare so you can choose with confidence, whoever you choose.

## What does a Shopify fulfillment company actually do?

A fulfillment company, also called a 3PL, stores your inventory, receives orders from your Shopify store automatically, picks and packs each order and ships it. Tracking numbers flow back to Shopify, which notifies your customer. Stock levels sync back too, so your storefront shows what is really available. Some providers also deliver locally with their own fleet; Kinematics does this across the GTA through our [final mile delivery](/services/final-mile-delivery) service.

## Why do lists of the "best" companies disagree?

Because "best" depends on the store. A provider that suits a high volume brand shipping across North America may be a poor fit for a growing brand whose customers are mostly in Ontario. The right choice depends on:

- **Where your customers are.** Local stock means faster local delivery.
- **What you sell.** Fragile, bulky or variant heavy products need different handling.
- **How many orders you ship**, and how much that changes through the year.
- **How much support you want.** Some brands want self serve tools; others want a person who knows their account.

## How should I compare Shopify fulfillment companies in Canada?

Use the same questions with every provider, and ask for answers in writing.

### How deep is the Shopify integration?

Ask exactly what syncs and how often: new orders, edits, cancellations, stock levels, fulfillment status and tracking numbers. Ask how bundles and variants are mapped, and what happens when an order is changed after it has been sent. Our [integrations](/integrations) page shows how this works with us.

### How fast can they deliver where your customers live?

Ask for delivery speeds by region, not a single national figure. If many of your customers are in the GTA, a provider with local delivery can offer next day as standard and [same day delivery](/services/same-day-delivery) when an order cannot wait.

### How accurate is their warehouse?

Ask how stock is received and counted, whether every SKU has its own bin location and whether picks are scanned. Mistakes in a warehouse turn into returns and support tickets for you.

### How do returns work?

Ask how returns come back, how they are graded and how quickly sellable items return to stock. A slow returns process quietly ties up inventory.

### Who answers when something goes wrong?

Ask who your day to day contact is and how quickly they respond. During peak season, a person who knows your account is worth a lot.

### Is the quote built on your real profile?

Pricing usually depends on storage, order volume, items per order, packaging and delivery areas. Share your actual numbers and compare quotes on the same basis. Check that each quote includes receiving, storage, packing, delivery and returns, not just a headline per order figure.

## What red flags should I watch for?

- **Vague answers about the integration.** If they cannot explain what syncs, assume problems.
- **A single delivery time for all of Canada.** Speed varies by region; good providers say so.
- **No written onboarding plan.** Moving stock without a plan leads to stockouts.
- **Quotes that leave things out.** Receiving, returns and special packaging often appear later.
- **Rankings as the main selling point.** Ask for process details instead.

## How do I switch providers without disrupting my store?

1. **Clean up product data first.** Unique SKUs, barcodes, weights and dimensions. Our guide to [Shopify fulfillment in Canada](/blog/shopify-fulfillment-in-canada) covers setup in detail.
2. **Plan inventory moves** so you never have zero stock available.
3. **Run test orders** to every shipping zone before going live.
4. **Watch the first weeks closely** and adjust packaging and cutoffs together.

## What should I ask on the first call?

Keep a short list and ask every provider the same questions, so the answers are easy to compare:

- What kind of brands do you work with today, and what volumes do they ship?
- Which Shopify data syncs, and how quickly do stock levels update after a pick or a return?
- What is your daily cutoff for packing, and when do parcels leave the building?
- Which carriers or fleets deliver my orders in each region, and what tracking do customers get?
- What proof of delivery do you capture, and how are failed deliveries handled?
- How are returns collected, graded and restocked, and how do I see what came back?
- Who is my day to day contact, and how do I reach them during a busy week?
- What does onboarding look like, step by step, with dates?

Write the answers down straight after each call. Differences that seem small in a sales conversation, such as a later packing cutoff or one more handoff between carriers, show up quickly in your reviews and support inbox.

## When does a provider with local delivery make sense?

If a large share of your orders go to one metro area, a provider that stores stock there and delivers locally removes the long haul leg and at least one handoff. Parcels can go from the packing bench to a van the same evening, which makes next day delivery realistic as a standard rather than a premium. Brands with customers spread evenly across the country may still be better served by a provider focused on national carriers, or by splitting stock between regions as volume grows.
