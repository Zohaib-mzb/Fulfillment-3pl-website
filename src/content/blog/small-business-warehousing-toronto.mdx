Most small brands in Toronto do not set out to run a warehouse. It starts with a few boxes in a spare room, then a corner of the garage, then a storage unit. At some point the inventory and the packing start running the business instead of the other way round. This guide walks through the usual warehousing options for a small business in Toronto and the GTA, and how to tell when it is time to hand storage and fulfillment to a partner.

## What are the warehousing options for a small business in Toronto?

There are four common stages, and many brands move through them in order.

### 1. Home or office

Storing stock at home costs nothing extra and keeps everything within reach. It works well while orders are occasional and products are small. The limits are space, time and the rest of your life: boxes in the hallway and evenings spent taping parcels.

### 2. Self storage

A storage unit adds space without a long commitment. It is useful for overflow and seasonal stock. The catch is that most units are built for storing things, not shipping them. Courier pickups, deliveries from suppliers and daily packing are awkward, so stock often has to be moved again before it is sent.

### 3. A rented unit or small warehouse

A small industrial unit gives you a real loading door, room for shelving and a place to pack. It also brings a lease, utilities, insurance, equipment and, sooner or later, staff. You are now running a small warehouse, with all the planning that involves.

### 4. A third party logistics provider (3PL)

A 3PL stores your inventory in its warehouse, picks and packs each order as it arrives from your store and ships it to your customer. You pay for the work and space you use rather than running the building. Our [fulfillment service](/services/fulfillment) is one example of this model, with our own delivery fleet for GTA orders.

## How do I know it is time to outsource?

There is no single order count that decides it. Look for these signs:

- **Packing has taken over someone's week**, usually the founder's.
- **Space has run out**, or stock is spread across several places.
- **Orders miss the courier pickup**, so "ships today" becomes "ships tomorrow".
- **Stock counts are guesses**, and you sell items you do not actually have.
- **A sale or launch would overwhelm** the people currently packing.
- **Customers ask where their order is** more often than you would like.

If two or three of these sound familiar, it is worth talking to a 3PL. A good provider will tell you honestly whether outsourcing makes sense yet.

## What should a small business look for in a Toronto warehouse partner?

When you compare providers, a few things matter more than any feature list.

### Do they work with brands your size?

Some providers focus on very large accounts. Ask directly who their typical customer is and how they handle a brand that ships a modest number of orders each day.

### How accurate is their receiving and picking?

Ask how inbound stock is counted against your shipment notice, whether every SKU gets its own bin location and whether picking is scanned against the order. Accuracy at the start prevents most problems later. The [3PL checklist](/blog/how-to-choose-a-3pl-in-toronto) goes deeper on these questions.

### How do GTA orders reach customers?

If many of your customers are local, ask who delivers them. A provider with its own [final mile delivery](/services/final-mile-delivery) can hand local parcels straight from the packing bench to a van, without waiting for a national carrier.

### How are returns handled?

Returns are part of selling online. Ask how returned items come back, how they are inspected and how quickly sellable stock is back on the shelf. See how [returns](/services/returns) work with us for one approach.

### Can you see your stock at any time?

You should be able to see live stock levels, order status and receiving history, and your store should update automatically.

## How is small business warehousing priced?

Pricing usually reflects storage space, the number of orders, items per order, packaging and delivery areas. Two brands with the same order count can have very different costs because their products and customers differ. That is why most providers, including Kinematics, quote each business individually. Share an honest picture of your volume, product sizes and seasonality, then compare quotes on the same basis, making sure each one includes receiving, storage, packing, delivery and returns.

## How do I prepare to move my inventory?

A smooth move comes down to good preparation:

1. **Clean up your product data.** Give every size and colour a unique SKU and record weights and dimensions.
2. **Decide on packaging.** Choose the boxes, mailers and inserts you want used, and how bundles are built.
3. **Connect your store.** Your provider will link your Shopify, WooCommerce or BigCommerce store so orders flow automatically. See [integrations](/integrations).
4. **Send a shipment notice.** List exactly what is arriving so it can be counted on receipt.
5. **Test before going live.** Place a few test orders and check that tracking reaches your customers.
