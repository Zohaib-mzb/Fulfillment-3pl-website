Handing your inventory to someone else is a big decision. Done well, it frees up your time, speeds up delivery and makes your brand look more professional. Done badly, it creates a new set of problems you can't see from your desk. This checklist walks through what to look at when you compare third party logistics (3PL) providers in Toronto and the wider GTA, and the questions that reveal how a warehouse really runs.

## When is it time to hire a 3PL?

There is no magic order count. The signals are usually practical:

- Packing orders has quietly become a full time job for someone who should be doing something else.
- Your stock has outgrown the spare room, the basement or the first rented unit.
- Orders miss the carrier pickup, so "ships today" becomes "ships tomorrow".
- Customers are writing in to ask where their order is.
- A sale or a launch would overwhelm the people currently packing.

If two or three of those sound familiar, it is worth starting conversations. A good 3PL will tell you honestly whether you are ready. If you are weighing storage options first, see [small business warehousing in Toronto](/blog/small-business-warehousing-toronto).

## Step 1: Start with where your orders go

Before comparing warehouses, look at your own order data. What share of your orders go to the Greater Toronto Area, the rest of Ontario, the rest of Canada and abroad?

If a large share of your customers are local, a Toronto area 3PL with its own GTA delivery can get those orders to doorsteps the next day without a long haul leg. If your customers are spread nationally, you will care more about how the 3PL hands parcels to national carriers. Most brands are a mix, which is why the next questions matter.

## Step 2: Check how stock is received and counted

Receiving is where inventory accuracy is won or lost. Ask:

- **Do you count inbound stock against a shipment notice?** You want discrepancies caught on day one, not discovered months later.
- **How quickly is stock put away and available to sell?** Inventory sitting on a dock isn't sellable.
- **Does every SKU get its own bin location?** Shared or "floating" locations lead to mispicks.
- **How are damaged units handled and reported?**

A 3PL that explains its receiving process clearly usually runs the rest of the warehouse with the same care. Our own approach is laid out on the [fulfillment services](/services/fulfillment) page if you want a reference point.

## Step 3: Ask about pick and pack standards and cutoffs

Next, understand what happens when an order arrives.

- **Is picking scan verified?** Scanning each item against the order is the simplest way to stop wrong items going out.
- **What is the daily cutoff for same day packing?** And is it realistic for when your orders actually come in?
- **Can they use your packaging and inserts?** Branded boxes, tissue, samples and thank you cards should go in the same way every time.
- **How do they handle kits, bundles and gift sets?**

Ask to see a packed order. It tells you more than any brochure.

## Step 4: Look closely at GTA delivery

This is where many brands get surprised. A warehouse can pack quickly, but if parcels then wait for a carrier pickup, travel to a hub and get handed to another network, "fast" fulfillment doesn't feel fast to the customer.

Ask:

- **Who delivers local orders?** The 3PL's own drivers, or another carrier after a handoff?
- **What does next day actually mean?** From which cutoff, to which postal codes?
- **Is there a same day option** for urgent orders?
- **What tracking do customers get?** A live link by text and email reduces "where is my order?" tickets.
- **What proof of delivery is captured?** A photo of where the parcel was left settles most disputes quickly.
- **What happens when a delivery fails?**

If local delivery matters to your brand, it is worth reading how [final mile delivery](/services/final-mile-delivery) works end to end, and comparing each provider against the same questions.

## Step 5: Understand the returns process

Returns are part of selling online, and slow returns cost money. Find out:

- How returns reach the warehouse: doorstep pickup, drop off or mail back.
- How items are inspected and graded.
- How quickly sellable items are back in stock.
- What reporting you get on reasons and conditions.

A clear [returns process](/services/returns) turns returned stock back into revenue quickly instead of leaving it in a pile.

## Step 6: Test the integration

Your store and the warehouse need to agree on what is in stock at all times.

- **Which platforms are supported?** Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, CSV, API?
- **What syncs back?** Stock levels, fulfillment status and tracking numbers should all flow to your store automatically.
- **How are bundles and variants mapped?**
- **What happens when an order is edited or cancelled after it is sent?**

Ask for a test order during onboarding and watch it go through the whole cycle.

## Step 7: Get pricing that reflects your real profile

3PL pricing is rarely one number. It usually reflects storage space, number of orders, items per order, packaging, special handling and delivery zones. Two brands with the same order count can have very different costs.

The most useful thing you can do is share an honest picture: monthly orders, average items per order, product sizes, seasonality and where your customers are. Then ask each provider for a custom quote based on that profile, and compare like for like. Be wary of quotes that look simple but leave out receiving, returns or delivery.

## Step 8: Meet the people you'll actually work with

Software matters, but people solve problems. Ask who you will talk to day to day, how quickly they respond, and what happens during peak season. A team that knows your account can explain a number in a dashboard, catch a problem early and suggest improvements you wouldn't have thought to ask for.

## A quick checklist to take into your calls

- Where do my orders go, and does this 3PL deliver locally itself?
- How is inbound stock counted, and how fast is it sellable?
- Is picking scan verified? What is the same day packing cutoff?
- Can they use my packaging, inserts and kits?
- What does next day mean here, and is same day available?
- What tracking and proof of delivery do customers get?
- How are returns collected, graded and restocked?
- Which integrations are supported, and what syncs back?
- Does the quote include receiving, storage, packing, delivery and returns?
- Who is my day to day contact?
