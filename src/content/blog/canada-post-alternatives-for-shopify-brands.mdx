Canada Post is the default for many Canadian Shopify stores, and for good reason: it reaches almost every address in the country and it is built into the Shopify shipping experience. But "default" doesn't mean "only". As brands grow, they often find that different parts of their order mix are better served by different options. This guide lays out the main alternatives neutrally, so you can decide what fits your store.

## Why do brands look beyond a single carrier?

It is rarely about one carrier being "bad". More often, a brand's needs become more specific:

- **Speed in a particular region.** Most of a brand's customers may be in one metro area, where a local network can deliver faster.
- **The tracking experience.** Brands want customers to see live updates and proof of delivery under one consistent experience.
- **Pickups that fit the day.** Scheduled pickups from your own location, timed around when you finish packing.
- **Cost structure.** Different services price by weight, size, zone or volume, and the right fit depends on your parcels.
- **Resilience.** Having more than one option means a busy season or service disruption doesn't stop your orders.

## Option 1: National couriers

National couriers offer broad coverage across Canada with a range of speeds, from economy to express. They are a natural fit when your customers are spread across provinces and you need one service that reaches everywhere.

**Worth checking:** pickup schedules, dimensional weight rules, residential surcharges, how delivery exceptions are handled and how tracking appears to your customer.

## Option 2: Regional and local last mile carriers

Regional carriers focus on a defined area, a city or a metro region, and specialize in the last leg to the door. Because their network is concentrated, they can often offer next day or same day delivery within that area and a more hands on service.

**Worth checking:** exactly which postal codes are covered, cutoffs, what happens to parcels outside the area, proof of delivery and how failed deliveries are handled. If most of your customers are in the GTA, our [final mile delivery](/services/final-mile-delivery) page shows what a local service can look like.

## Option 3: Shipping platforms and label tools

Shipping platforms connect to several carriers and let you compare rates, buy labels and print them in one place. They don't deliver parcels themselves; they give you choice and convenience.

**Worth checking:** which carriers are included, how rates are shown at checkout, whether the platform syncs tracking back to Shopify, and any monthly or per label fees.

## Option 4: A 3PL with its own local delivery

A third party logistics provider stores your stock and packs your orders. Some also run their own local delivery fleet, so local orders go from the packing bench straight to a delivery van without a handoff. Orders outside that region are typically passed to a national carrier.

**Worth checking:** whether the 3PL actually delivers locally itself, how inventory syncs with Shopify, what tracking and proof customers get, and how returns work. Our [fulfillment services](/services/fulfillment) page describes one version of this model.

## Option 5: Local delivery and pickup from your own location

Shopify includes local delivery and local pickup settings for merchants with a physical location. For a shop delivering to nearby neighbourhoods, or customers happy to collect, it can work well at small volumes.

**Worth checking:** whether you have the people and vehicles to deliver consistently, and what happens when volume grows.

## How do I set up multiple carriers in Shopify?

Shopify's shipping settings are flexible enough to run a mixed setup:

1. **Create shipping zones by destination.** For example, a zone for GTA postal codes and another for the rest of Canada.
2. **Assign rates and services to each zone.** Offer next day or same day in the local zone and a national service elsewhere.
3. **Name rates clearly.** "Next day delivery (GTA)" tells the customer exactly what they are choosing.
4. **Connect fulfillment and tracking.** Make sure tracking numbers sync back so customers get updates from your store.
5. **Test with real addresses** in each zone before going live.

## Questions to ask any shipping option

Whichever direction you lean, the same questions apply:

- Which postal codes do you cover, and how fast?
- What is the cutoff for that speed?
- Do you collect from my location, or do I drop off?
- What tracking does my customer see, and is there proof of delivery?
- What happens when a delivery fails?
- How do returns work?
- How is pricing calculated for my parcel sizes?

## What about returns and exchanges?

Shipping options don't end at the customer's door. When you compare alternatives, look at how returns come back as well. Some options rely on the customer printing a label and dropping a parcel off; others can collect returns from the door within their delivery area. If you sell products with frequent exchanges, the return leg can matter as much as the outbound one.

It is also worth checking how each option shares return tracking with you, so your team knows when a returned item is on its way and can plan refunds or exchanges. Our [returns](/services/returns) page explains one approach for GTA customers.

## A balanced conclusion

There's no single right answer. Canada Post remains a strong choice for wide national reach and for rural and remote addresses. Couriers, regional carriers, shipping platforms and 3PLs each earn their place in different parts of an order mix. The brands that do this well usually match each region to the option that serves it best, and keep the customer experience consistent across all of them.
